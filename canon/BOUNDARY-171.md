# CUBI 171 — Continuous charged torus, necessary conditions

This is an explicit conditional test of a **classical** continuous toroidal shell, not a general proof about every possible toroidal field or quantum electron structure. Let major radius R, minor radius a (0<a<R), total uniform-signed electric charge Q=-e distributed uniformly over the torus surface, and an azimuthal rigid angular velocity Omega about the z-axis. Torus surface area weighting gives mean cylindrical radius squared E[s²]=R²+(3/2)a². Current magnetic moment magnitude is |mu|=e |Omega| E[s²]/2, and the speed bound at outer edge gives |Omega|<=c/(R+a). Hence |mu|<=e c (R²+1.5a²)/(2(R+a)). Compare with electron |mu_e|=|g_e|e*hbar/(4m_e), g_e≈2.002319304.

Outside any enclosing sphere of radius b=R+a, the vacuum static electric field's energy satisfies U_E,out>=Q²/(8*pi*epsilon0*b) by Gauss' law and Cauchy–Schwarz. For b of order Planck length this vastly exceeds the electron rest energy m_e*c². This is an unconditional bound *within the assumed classical vacuum electrostatic framework*, not an applicable bound to all quantum field configurations. Magnetic field and mechanical stresses add complications and cannot cancel positive EM energy for an isolated conventional Maxwell field. A rotating field is not automatically in static equilibrium; this is an upper bound/necessary-condition audit rather than a Maxwell–stress dynamical solution.
