# CUBI 162 — Ideal Magnetic Dipole Curvature

For a vacuum magnetic point dipole (outside its source), field lines satisfy `r=L sin²(theta)` in a meridional plane. Using plane-curve polar curvature `|r²+2(r')²-r r''|/[r²+(r')²]^(3/2)`, at the magnetic equator `theta=pi/2`, `r=L`, `r'=0`, `r''=-2L`, giving `kappa=3/L`.

At radii 9, 6, 3, 1, equatorial field-line curvatures are 1/3, 1/2, 1 and 3 in reciprocal model-length units. **PASS**: monotonic increase at equatorial points on *different* ideal dipole field lines. **NOT TESTED**: monotonic increase all along any one field line. **BOUNDARY**: r=0 is singular in the point-dipole idealization. **UNVERIFIED**: weak=9, medium=6, strong=3 and fission=1 correspond to actual physical force strengths or transitions. Nuclear fission is not a fundamental force.

This is a geometrical consequence of an ideal Maxwell dipole, not measurement of electron geometry. The source field is not established by the toroidal model, and real source geometry, boundaries and particle physics require further work. `dipole162.js` recomputes and labels the analytic test.
